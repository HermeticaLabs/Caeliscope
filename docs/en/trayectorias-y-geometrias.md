## Trajectories

The **Trajectory** button on an object's card draws its path across the sky in its own color. You can leave several on. Each appears as a chip that lets you remove it.

The **range** is chosen in Options → Trajectory range:

| Range | Shows |
|---|---|
| ±12 hours | Your sky: what it travels in half a day each way. |
| ±3 months | Against the stars: planets' retrograde loops. |
| ±6 months | The same, longer. |
| Synodic cycle | The full cycle relative to the Sun (Moon and planets). |
| Rosette | The planet's path against the stars over its full cycle of years. |
| Analemma | The Sun at the same clock time every day, over a year. |
| Saros | 18 years and 11 days: the eclipse repeat cycle. |

With **Trajectory labels** on you will see dates along the path and the retrograde stations (℞ at the start, D when it turns direct again).

## Geometry window

Long cycles open in a geometry window that is drawn while the engine computes it (with a progress percentage). When it finishes you can:

- **Replay** the drawing animation.
- Switch to **3D**: an interactive view of the full cycle. Drag to rotate, wheel to zoom, double-click to center. The plane buttons (**Top**, **Front**, **Side**) fix an orthogonal view, and **Center** restores the default view.
- Exaggerate the height (**Z ×n**) to better see the tilt; **Z ×1 (true)** returns to the true scale.

The figures are seen from the Earth (+), fixed to the stars, and retrograde motion is painted in another color. In the **Saros** the Moon circles the Earth with the Sun fixed on the right: 223 lunations, one loop per month; eclipses happen where the loop crosses the Sun line near the plane.

## Star trails

From a star's card, **All** draws the trails of all bright stars like a long-exposure photograph: live as time passes, or the full cycle at once.
