## Find event

On the time bar, **Find event** jumps to the next or previous:

- **Solar eclipse** (total, partial or annular).
- **Lunar eclipse** (total, partial or penumbral).
- **New Moon.**
- **Equinox or solstice** (March, June, September and December).

With **Only eclipses visible from your location** on, the search ignores eclipses that cannot be seen from the chosen place. If there is none in the searched range, Caeliscope says so, with the years checked.

On arriving at an eclipse, the result gives you the instant of maximum, the magnitude, and for a solar eclipse the percentage of the disc covered (or **TOTALITY**), and whether the Sun or the Moon is below the horizon at that instant.

## During an eclipse

When the shown instant falls inside an eclipse, an on-screen notice states its type and magnitude. In a lunar eclipse the brightness of the darkened Moon depends on the **Danjon scale** (0, very dark, to 4, bright), which you can adjust in Options.

## Shadow guide

Turn on **Options → Shadow guide** to see the shadow cones of the current instant:

- **Solar eclipse:** a side view with exaggerated widths and, below, the shadow on the plane through the centre of the Earth, to scale, seen from the Sun. It says whether the axis touches the Earth (total or annular) or only the penumbra (partial), the axis distance from the centre, and the umbra and penumbra widths.
- **Lunar eclipse:** the Moon against the Earth's umbra (dark) and penumbra (light), to scale, as seen in the sky (east to the left), with the umbral and penumbral magnitudes.

Together with the **Saros** cycle (see [Trajectories and geometries](trayectorias-y-geometrias.md)), the guide helps you understand why an eclipse happens when it does.
