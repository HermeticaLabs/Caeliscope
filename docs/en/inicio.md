Caeliscope is an immersive sky viewer that runs in the browser. It shows the real sky from any place and moment: stars, constellations, the Milky Way, the Sun, the Moon, the planets and their moons, with eclipses, trajectories and Solar System geometries.

It is a **demo of Caelis Engine**, Hermetica Labs' deterministic astronomical engine. Caelis Engine computes every position; Caeliscope only draws them. What you see on screen is what the engine returns, and you can check it in the JSON view of each object.

## Your first 60 seconds

1. **Open the demo** at [hermeticalabs.github.io/Caeliscope](https://hermeticalabs.github.io/Caeliscope/). It starts with the logo sequence; click anywhere or press any key to continue.
2. **Choose where you are.** The globe appears: drag it to turn it, use the wheel or pinch to zoom, and click to move the pin. You can also press **My location** or type the latitude and longitude.
3. **Press "See the sky from here".** The view switches to the sky of the chosen place, at the current instant.
4. **Look around.** Drag to turn the view, wheel or pinch to zoom. Click an object to see its card.
5. **Travel in time.** The bottom bar steps the clock forward or back, or jumps to any date.

## What you need

- A current desktop, tablet or phone browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) with WebGL 2 and hardware acceleration enabled. Any modern GPU, integrated or dedicated, is enough.
- Nothing to install and no account to create. The interface language is English or Spanish and is changed in **Options**.

## Install it as an app

Caeliscope can be installed as an app and opened **offline** after the first visit. Open **About** (click the logo in the top-left corner) and press the install button, or use your browser's "Install" option.

Offline, everything works except the satellite detail of the globe, which comes from NASA GIBS and needs internet. The globe then uses its local textures.
